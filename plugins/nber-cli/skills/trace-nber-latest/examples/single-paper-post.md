# The Labor Market Impacts of GLP-1s

## One-liner
GLP-1 treatment reduces long-term sickness leave by 17.3% among Danish patients, but has no significant impact on income, employment, or labor force participation within four years.

## Abstract
We estimate the causal impacts of GLP-1 treatment on labor market outcomes using linked Danish administrative data and a matched stacked difference-in-differences design. We compare patients who initiate GLP-1 treatment during the first two years of Semaglutide availability to observably similar patients who initiate four years later. We find that GLP-1 treatment reduces long-term sickness leave by 17.3%. We estimate total fiscal benefits of GLP-1 initiation of approximately 1.3–1.5% of annual labor income per employed individual. We do not detect statistically significant or economically meaningful impacts on income, labor force participation, or employment over four years.

## Why read it?
If you care about the intersection of health and labor economics, this paper offers the first large-scale causal evidence on how GLP-1 drugs affect real labor market outcomes using administrative data.

## Story
The authors compare patients who started GLP-1 treatment during the first two years of Semaglutide availability with observably similar patients who started four years later. The key finding is that GLP-1s substantially reduce long-term sickness absence, generating fiscal benefits worth 1.3–1.5% of annual labor income per employed person. However, the treatment does not move the needle on employment rates, labor force participation, or earnings over the four-year window.

### Shine point
Rare causal evidence from linked administrative data; clear separation between health benefits (fewer sick days) and economic margins (no employment or income effects yet); directly relevant to the debate on whether GLP-1s will reshape the labor market.
